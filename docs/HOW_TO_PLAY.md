# How to play (onboarding source)

Living list of everything a player can do. Each player-facing feature adds or updates its entry
in the PR that ships it (CLAUDE.md, Workflow). Phase 5 builds the interactive tutorial and the
in-game "How to play" screen from this list (ROADMAP Phase 5), so a feature without an entry here
is a feature players will not learn.

**Teach** marks what a new player will not find alone: the tutorial must show it, not only the
help screen. Timings and sizes live in `data/`, so entries name the setting instead of a number.

**Id** is the first column of every table. A new row gets `<section prefix>-<slug>`: the prefix is
the section heading in kebab case (`turn-the-phone`), the slug is 1-4 words naming the action, in
plain text with no backticks, at most 48 characters, unique in this file. An id is never reused
and never changed; when a row is deleted, add its id to `RETIRED` in
`tests/project/test_how_to_play_ids.gd`. The help text in `data/help/how_to_play.tres` names rows by id.

## Camera
| Id | Action | Result | Teach |
|---|---|---|---|
| camera-pan | Drag one finger | Pans the map | |
| camera-pinch | Pinch | Zooms about the fingers; on release it settles on the nearest of three zoom levels (`zoom_levels` in `data/input_settings.tres`) | |
| camera-look-diamond | Look at the map | The map is drawn at an angle, as a diamond: every tile is a diamond and the map's corners point up, down, left and right. Drag, pinch and taps work as before, and a tap on the ground sends the units to the point under your finger | |
| camera-map-edge | Drag the map to its edge | The camera stops at the map's edge: the middle of the screen never leaves the map. The edge can reach the middle of the screen, so what lies under the BUILD button, the build drawer, the group column or the panels at the top can be scrolled into view; beyond the edge the screen is dark grey. Turning the phone keeps the middle of the screen where it was | |
| camera-radar | Look at the round radar at the top right | The ground round your view: a small round window, drawn at the same angle as the map, with a white frame for what the screen shows. Tap it and the whole map unfolds under it, as a small diamond like the map itself (on a very short screen it unfolds from the top instead, to the left of the radar and of the strike button above BUILD, if you have one; if you keep it open with its pin while you aim, it stands left of the strike button's check mark and X too, and gets a little narrower to leave the pill its room; on a very short screen where even that does not fit, a pinned map folds when you start to aim, and the radar opens it again after the aim). Ground you have never seen is black and ground you saw before is dimmed. Your buildings and units show in your colour, as small marks the size of what they stand on; the enemy's show only where you see them now, in its colour, and bigger and rounder, so you can tell them apart without colour: an enemy unit is a small cross, an enemy building a rounded blob. A building you saw and then lost sight of stays as a dimmed blob until you see that spot again. Tap or drag on the full map and the view jumps there: the middle of the screen goes to the ground point under your finger, and a drag keeps following it; a touch beside the diamond lands on the nearest edge of the map. With units selected, hold it to send them there (see the next row). It folds away by itself after a few seconds without a touch (`radar_close_ms` in `data/input_settings.tres`), unless you tap its pin (the thumbtack, left of the arrows button in the head of the map; it stays pinned until you tap it again, and for the rest of the match); the arrows button folds it at once. A touch on the battlefield (drag, pinch, tap, hold) folds an unpinned map while the touch does its work as always (a pinned map stays); a touch on the map itself never folds it, and a touch on a button folds it only when that button opens something else. The map and the build drawer are one at a time, sideways and upright: opening the drawer (BUILD, tapping your Headquarters, barracks or vehicle factory, or the drawer coming back after a placement you began in it) folds the map, pinned or not, and the pin stays; opening the map slides the drawer away. While your radar building is switched off by a Dusk Array the round radar goes dark and the full map folds (see `superweapon-blackout-radar`) | yes |
| camera-hold-full-map | Hold the full map with units selected | Keep a finger still on the full map for `long_press_ms` (`data/input_settings.tres`) and the selected units move to the ground point under it (a finger resting there keeps the map from folding): the same move a ground tap sends, with the chevrons where they are going and a short buzz. It is always a move: tankers held over an oil or gas field drive there but do not load (tap the field on the map for that), and after a few seconds they go back to the field they worked (`harvest_resume_ticks` in `data/rules.tres`). The view has jumped there already, so you see them go. One hold is one order, however long you keep resting; lift and hold again for another. A short tap, or a finger that slides (it moves the view instead), orders nothing, and neither does a hold with nothing or only a building selected, or while you place a building or aim a strike. A hold beside the diamond sends them to the nearest edge of the map, and the chevrons are there. Turning the phone, or the full map folding away, while your finger is down cancels the hold | yes |

## Turn the phone
| Id | Action | Result | Teach |
|---|---|---|---|
| turn-the-phone-rotate | Turn the phone, on a menu or during a match | The game plays in portrait and in landscape and follows the phone's sensor. A match goes on while you turn: nothing restarts and no order is given, the map keeps its centre and the size of what is on it, and the controls keep their size and move to the layout of the other orientation | |
| turn-the-phone-cancel-gesture | Turn the phone while a finger is on the screen (a drag, a box or a pinch) | That gesture is cancelled, never turned into a tap or an order: no unit is selected or sent. Lift the finger and start the gesture again | |
| turn-the-phone-keep-state | Turn the phone with the full map or the build drawer open | Each keeps its state through the turn and moves to its place in the other orientation: the full map under the radar, the drawer from the right edge sideways or from the bottom upright, still on the tab you had. A drawer that was closed stays closed | |
| turn-the-phone-auto-rotate-off | Auto-rotate is off in the phone's settings | The game stays in the orientation the phone is in. The game cannot force a turn and has no lock of its own | |

The fighter is the faster and cheaper one, never runs out of shots and hits high-flying aircraft only (not drone squads). The bomber is slower and tougher, hits the ground only (units and buildings) and carries two bombs (`ammo` in `data/units/`; see Orders, "A bomber has dropped its two bombs") | yes |

| units-build-carrier | Build a ground carrier (Vehicles tab of the build drawer: Nordværn's Bunk Wagon, Facet's Linecar) | Queued and built like any vehicle at a vehicle factory (`cost` and `build_ticks` in `data/units/`, each carrier with its own values). The Bunk Wagon is tracked, slow, tough (heavy armour) and has four seats; the Linecar is wheeled, fast, light and has two seats (`carry_slots` in `data/units/`) | yes |

| units-build-minelayer | Build a minelayer (Vehicles tab of the build drawer: Nordværn's Shot Truck, Facet's Seeder; each needs your faction's radar) | Queued and built like any vehicle at a vehicle factory (`cost` and `build_ticks` in `data/units/`, each minelayer with its own values). A minelayer has no gun and never sets off a mine, yours or an enemy's (`mine_aware`). It finds hidden enemy mines within `detect_milli` of itself. The Shot Truck is slower and sturdier, the Seeder fast and light (`speed` and `max_hp` in `data/units/`). Each mine it lays costs `mine_cost` credits | yes |
| units-look-minelayer | Look at a minelayer | The Shot Truck is a low olive flatbed truck on six dark wheels with a boxed cab, a long steel drill mast laid slantwise on its bed and four steel shot cans with a team-colour band and stud beside it; the Seeder is a light blue-grey four-wheeled planter with a small glass-fronted cab, a tall hexagonal glass hopper behind it and a jointed arm at the tail that drops a white spike. A medic squad carries no weapon and cannot attack. The Patchers are slower and sturdier and mend one squad at a time; the Menders are quicker and more fragile, reach further and mend two at once (`speed`, `max_hp` and `repair_slots` in `data/units/`). | buildings-look | Look at a building | Each building is drawn from the picture set of its side in its player's team colour, standing on its footprint with a soft shadow on the ground. ## Selection
| Id | Action | Result | Teach |
|---|---|---|---|
| selection-tap | Tap an own unit | Selects that unit only. Tap anywhere on its picture, the middle of it included, not only on the ground under its feet: a unit is hit round the middle of its picture, and where two are close the one whose middle is nearer your finger is picked. A unit is easy to hit: at least 48 dp across (`hit_radius_dp` in `data/input_settings.tres`), however small it is drawn. Selected units show a white oval ring lying on the ground under them, as big as the unit's circle on the ground and never smaller than 36 dp across | |
| selection-tap-building | Tap your own building | Selects that building only, and a unit standing at the same spot is picked instead of it. A building is easy to hit: at least 48 dp across (`hit_radius_dp` in `data/input_settings.tres`), however small it is drawn. It gets a frame of four corner brackets and its health bar, the command bar's chip shows its picture with the health bar under it (no name). Mend shows for any own finished building and Sell for all of them except the Headquarters, neither on a construction site. A Headquarters, barracks, vehicle factory, airfield or engineering centre does not open the build drawer | |
| selection-tap-enemy-building | Tap an enemy building you can see, with nothing selected | Selects it for its information only: an amber frame and its notched health bar show, and the command bar's chip shows its picture and health, no name. | selection-tap-ground | Tap the ground with any building selected except a barracks or a vehicle factory | Lets go of the building (also an enemy building you looked at). A barracks or a vehicle factory is different: the same tap sets its rally flag (see Orders) and it stays selected | yes |
| selection-double-tap | Double-tap an own unit | Selects every own unit of that type on screen. A building never joins a double-tap, a box or "All army" | yes |
| selection-tap-aircraft | Tap an own aircraft (a fighter, a bomber, a transport or a drone squad) | Selects it. An aircraft hovers above its shadow, so tap the aircraft itself, not its shadow: a selected aircraft wears the white ring on the ground under its shadow and a health bar above it, as a ground unit does. ## Orders
| Id | Action | Result | Teach |
|---|---|---|---|
| orders-stop | Select units, then tap Stop (the command bar, after the chip) | The units stop where they are and stand idle; the phone gives a light buzz (`haptic_ms` in `data/input_settings.tres`, if haptics are on). Stop shows for units only; one own finished building has Mend instead, and Sell unless it is the Headquarters (see Buildings). It works while you place a building or aim a strike too. A tanker you stopped goes back to work by itself after a few seconds (`harvest_resume_ticks` in `data/rules.tres`). There is no Attack button: tap the ground to move and an enemy to attack | yes |
| orders-home | Select aircraft, then tap Home (the command bar, after Stop) | Each selected aircraft flies to the pad of its own airfield, where it reloads and is repaired for credits, and waits. An aircraft whose airfield has no free pad, or that has no airfield, is left out.
