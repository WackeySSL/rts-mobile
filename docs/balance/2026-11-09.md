## 1. Main finding: the seat decides every set between two thinking AIs

The top seat wins almost every match between two thinking AIs:

| Set | Pairing, scenario | N | Top seat win share (per-mille) | Bottom seat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| tuning | normal,normal, cross | 100 | 980 | 20 |
| fossil-mirror | normal,normal, fossil-mirror | 20 | 1000 | 0 |
| renew-mirror | normal,normal, renew-mirror | 20 | 1000 | 0 |
| ladder-easy | easy,normal, cross | 20 | 950 | 50 |
| ladder-hard | hard,normal, cross | 20 | 975 | 25 |
| baseline | dummy,easy, cross | 10 | 500 | 500 |

(A draw or a timeout counts half to each side.) The mirrors are the clean test: same faction,
same level, same units, and the bottom seat wins 0 of 40. In the 100-match tuning set the faction
split is 480/520, so faction is not what decides; the seat is (980/20).

Easy beats the dummy from either seat, so a seat edge, if any, is smaller than the gap between
Easy and the dummy. This run cannot tell a map cause from an AI cause; P195 diagnoses it.

What this means for reading the rest of the report: faction win shares, level win shares (easy
450 vs normal 550 in ladder-easy, hard 525 vs normal 475 in ladder-hard) and the
kills-per-1000-credits and survival columns cannot be read as unit or level strength while one
seat wins about 98% of matches. The level shares in the two ladders are the seat share seen
through which level sat at the top: the ladders run both level orders (`easy,normal` and
`normal,easy`; `hard,normal` and `normal,hard`) and each lands near 50% per level only because
the seat flips between the two shards. Kills and survival mix the winner's and the loser's rows.
They are listed in section 3 only so the next report has a before-value.

Row P195 (the seat bias; in progress on 2026-11-09, not yet in the plan) diagnoses the cause. The
lead holds tuning rows (P192) until its fix.

## 5. Findings that repeat across sets

The findings below repeat across sets. Only the baseline is seat-balanced, and it plays Easy
against the dummy, so this run cannot fully separate them from the seat bias.

- Production mix. The share per type is nearly identical in tuning (576/207/103/63/35/17),
  fossil-mirror (579/209/97/62/36/18) and renew-mirror (572/200/109/77/27/15). The tier-1 role
  weights (Normal's build_order_tier 1) in `data/ai/opponent_*.tres` are 35 (anti-infantry), 30
  (anti-vehicle), 25 (tank), 10 (artillery), yet rifle squads are about 57% of units made and
  tanks about 6-7%. The baseline, where the seat does not decide, is rifle-heavy too (764 and 734
  per-mille on Easy's 50/40 weights). The draw picks only among roles that can be queued now
  (room in the tab's queue, affordable; AiProduction.gd), and Normal moves 30% of the weight to
  counters (counter_percent). Which of these makes the gap is not diagnosed here. P195 or a
  later AI row should look at the draw; a weight change alone is not a justified proposal yet.
- Harvesters, tankers and scouts sit at 1.5-3.6% of production in the three Normal-vs-Normal
  sets. Normal caps harvesters at harvesters_per_refinery 2 on refineries_max 2 (the free tanker
  included) and sends a scout every scout_interval_ticks 2700, so low shares are expected.
  ADR 0031 (answer 2 A) still flags every type under 5%; whether these three keep that flag is a
  question for the lead, not a tuning proposal.
- Match length. Even the baseline, where the seat does not decide, has a 10.8 min median (9693
  ticks; longest 11510): Easy fights a dummy that builds turrets and defends but never attacks.
  Normal vs normal medians (11779-12468 ticks) end soon after Normal's `all_in_tick` (10800):
  the matches seem to be decided by the all-in, not by the waves. The baseline median (9693)
  ends before Easy's all_in_tick 12600, so the all-in is not the only way matches end. This is
  a reading of the medians, not a measurement of when each match's last building fell.
- Every type is used. In the tuning, mirror and ladder sets each unit type of each faction was
  produced (smallest: ladder-easy light_tank 5, renew_scout 13). The baseline's artillery and
  scout zeros follow Easy's data; its tank zeros are not explained (section 3).

## 8. Proposals

At most three, each one number in `data/`, only for findings the seat cannot explain. It waits for
P195: no number should change while one seat wins about 98% of matches, since the next run could
not show its effect.

1. After P195 (hypothesis, to be tested one number at a time): `data/ai/difficulty/normal.tres`
   `all_in_tick` 10800 -> 7200. Evidence: Normal vs normal medians end about 1000-1700 ticks after
   10800 (section 5), and the median is 13.3 min against the 8-10 min target. Expected effect: if
   matches still end 1000-1700 ticks after the all-in, medians move to about 8200-8900 ticks
   (9.1-9.9 min) and the timeouts drop. Risk: it also moves Normal's behaviour against Easy and
   Hard; compare the ladders on the same seeds. It puts Normal's all-in before Hard's (9900;
   ADR 0030 §6 orders Easy 12600 > Normal 10800 > Hard 9900). The row also changes the pin in
   tests/ai/test_opponent_def_data.gd and the script default in game/ai/AiDifficultyDef.gd, which
   a data test holds equal to Normal's file; exception 20 does not name that default, so the lead
   decides.

No second or third proposal. The production mix (rifle 57%) is the biggest finding that repeats
across sets, but its cause is not a data number: the role weights (35/30/25/10) do not match the
realised mix, so changing one weight would be a guess. The harvester, tanker and scout shares
follow Normal's caps and scout timer (section 5).
