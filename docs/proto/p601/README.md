# P601 style prototype (exploration, never merged)

Question from Michael (2026-11-24): can our own pipeline (`tools/artgen/`, ADR 0023) move toward the
gritty, military Figma mood? One unit and one building were redrawn: the heavy tank (`heavy_tank`,
Bullhead) and a Helix-style command hold (replacing `renew_hq`). The Figma pictures were looked at
for mood only; nothing was imported, traced or colour-picked. The models are built from the words
of the style (heavy steel modules, wide tracks, large orange panels, hazard stripes, dark gunmetal;
faceted low buildings, closed armour, large blue panels with darker trim). The blue triad
(`#68A8F2 #2F6FD0 #1B4A9C`) and the extra tones (`gunmetal #4B5256`, `darksteel`, `wornsteel`,
`rubber`, `hazard #D9B222`, `trim`, `glow`) are our own picks in `ArtPalette.GRIT`.

Silhouettes (own words). Tank: a low, wide slab of dark gunmetal on two broad tracks with
hazard-striped end plates, a stepped orange deck and glacis, a faceted orange turret with a rear
bustle and a long gun with a hazard band. Building: a low eight-sided armoured hall of stepped,
sloped blue panels with navy trim bands on a gunmetal apron, a crowned sensor spire, four corner
pylons and a hazard-edged hangar gate.

## What changed in artgen (branch only)

- `ArtDetail.gd` (new): a second renderer over the same `ArtPrim`/`ArtMesh` faces. Output is still
  only `svg`, `g`, `polygon`; no gradient, filter, clip or text. Levels: L0 flat three tones;
  L1 continuous face shading (about 15 tones from the face normal instead of 3); L2 every large quad
  face split into panel cells with a dark seam gap, a per-cell tone jitter, bolt dots at cell
  corners, hazard stripes (diagonal bands clipped to the face), octagon caps split into a ring of
  panels; L3 bevel strips (light top edge, dark lower edge), stacked translucent contact bands
  along the foot of vertical faces (the fake ambient occlusion), paint chips at panel edges, grime
  streaks. A six-ring soft ground shadow replaces the three-ring pad.
- `ArtPalette.gd`: seven shared "grit" materials and `key_override` (so Helix panels can be blue).
- `gen_proto_p601.gd`: driver (run with `godot --headless --script ... -- SCRATCH_DIR`); writes SVG frames
  at all four levels, exports faces as JSON, rasterises the SVGs through Godot's own SVG loader
  (what the match-start bake does) and records `stats.json`.
- `proto_p601_bake.py` (the PNG path): shades the exported faces per pixel in numpy, 3x
  supersampled: seams, bolts, stripes, chips, grime, Lambert plus sky term, a small specular lobe
  on metals, a real screen-space ambient occlusion pass over a 3D position buffer (so the turret
  base, the glacis and the track links occlude each other), outlines and edge wear.
- `proto_p601_models.py`: writes the models. Loops make the track links, grille slats, wheels.
- `proto_p601_preview.py`, `proto_p601_finish.py`: compose the sheets.
- Nothing under `assets/art/iso/`, `tests/` or the real models was changed; `bash scripts/gen-art.sh`
  output is untouched. `verify.sh` was not run (exploration). The `ArtPalette` additions are
  additive, but a real change would need the key-band tests and `ArtModel.problems` updated for the
  new materials.

## Cost per unit versus today (heavy tank, all 16 hull and 16 turret directions plus shadows)

| | Today | Proto SVG (L3) | Proto PNG bake |
|---|---|---|---|
| Model | about 36 prims | 138 prims | same model |
| Polygons, 48 frames | 2,693 | 18,409 (6.8x; L2: 13,854; L0: 9,626) | none (pixels) |
| Disk, 48 frames | 287 KB SVG | 1,698 KB SVG (5.9x; L2 1,268 KB; L0 951 KB) | 442 KB RGBA PNG for hull+turret at 1.0 (125 KB as 256-colour palette PNG, visible banding risk); shadows stay SVG |
| Canvas, hull / turret / shadow (2x art px) | 166x104 / 214x118 / 248x128 | 158x112 / 220x120 / 242x124 | same |
| Pixels to rasterise at scale 1.0, 48 frames | 1.19 M | 1.19 M | 0 at match start (0.71 M px shipped for hull+turret) |
| Match-start bake of the 48 frames (headless Godot, one run, noisy) | 57 ms | 164 ms (about 3x) | PNG decode only |
| Atlas texture memory | unchanged: it follows canvas size and `bake_scale`, not polygon count | same | same |
| Draw calls per unit | 3 sprites (hull, turret, shadow), one atlas | unchanged | unchanged |

Reading for rule 6 (150 units at 60 fps): the per-frame cost on the phone does not change in
either path. The sprites have the same canvases, the same count and the same atlas, so draw calls
and fill rate stay. What grows is (a) the one-off bake at match start in the SVG path (about 3x
per unit type, to be measured on a real mid-range phone in CI; a roster of about 12 unit types
and 16 buildings is a few hundred ms to about a second), (b) the shipped size, and (c) the
parse memory of larger SVG text. The building is the heavy one: 210 KB of SVG for one frame
(2,446 polygons, 800 of them bolts), 90 KB as PNG. Cut bolts from buildings (or draw them only at
L3 on the nearest faces) before shipping anything like this. At 8 directions per vehicle instead of
16 all disk and bake numbers halve.

Web export size: SVG text compresses roughly 8 to 10x in the pck, so the 1.7 MB tank is a few
hundred KB; the whole roster at this level would add on the order of 10 to 20 MB to the pck
(estimate, not measured). A PNG path would cost about the same on disk with no bake time but
fixes the resolution at export time (zoom 1.5 on a 3x phone would blur unless baked at 3x: 2.25x
the pixels, about 1 MB per tank in RGBA).

## What it would take to do all units and buildings this way

1. Decide the colour question first (it is an architecture question, not an art one): the Figma
   orange panels are large and are in our fossil key band. Here they are the team triad (`team`),
   so the player recolours the whole panel set; that breaks the 3 to 20 % team share test and the
   enemy/ally read of a tank that is 60 % key colour. The alternative is fixed faction colour
   panels and a different, small team marker (a white or yellow-green band), which means moving
   the key band and rewriting `ArtPalette` key tests, the bake and the gallery triads.
2. Move the detail renderer into the real generator behind a per-model `detail` level so
   `--check` stays deterministic (everything here is hash-seeded, byte-stable). Add tests for the
   new output (polygon budget per frame, only svg/g/polygon).
3. Modelling: the tank took 138 primitives against 36, 4x the lines. 24 models per faction in the
   roster means about 50 models; with macros (tracks, wheel sets, grilles, pylons, hatches) as
   generator helpers rather than lines, about 1 to 2 hours each, so one to two weeks of art
   direction work for the set. Model the faction look as reusable macros first.
4. Generator features the prototype lacked: a chamfered box and rotated primitives (everything is
   axis-aligned, so panels cannot be angled; the Helix building reads as a dome, not as crisp
   facets, because only 8-gon frustums were available); face decals as part of the model
   (numbers, hazard decals, vents); per-prim detail override (no bolts on small parts).
5. Hull/turret occlusion: turret and hull are separate frames, so the turret cannot occlude the
   hull in the PNG path (the SVG path fakes it with the deck colour). A baked contact band on
   the hull under the turret ring is needed.
6. Pick the ship format. SVG keeps ADR 0023 and the resolution independence; PNG gives the
   per-pixel look but not a recolourable team key (key colours have to be a mask layer or a
   second atlas channel, because the bake replaces key hex strings in SVG text).

## Honest assessment

The reference is a lit, textured render. Our pipeline reaches the mood but not the finish: about
70 percent of the way for the tank, about 55 percent for the building. What carries over well:
the heavy, wide, panelled proportions; orange panels on dark gunmetal; hazard stripes; track links
and skirts; the seams and bolts that make panels read as plates; a soft contact shadow; blue
facets with navy trim. What does not: smooth specular gradients on metal, subtle surface
variation, greebles smaller than a pixel at game zoom, and global illumination. At the game's
real zoom (a tank 100 to 150 px wide) the difference to a 2.5x render shrinks a lot, and the
32 px greyscale check shows both new variants read as a tank and a building; the PNG bake holds
contrast against sand better, the SVG's mid-orange is close in luminance to sand, so the dark
tracks and the shadow do the work there.

The PNG bake looks grittier, but it is noisier at far zoom, and its main extra over L3 SVG is the
real ambient occlusion and the per-pixel grime. L3 SVG gets most of the way at 1/4 of the
effort to ship (no new pipeline). Recommendation for the architect: SVG at L2 for units and L3 for
nearby hero buildings, drop the bolt dots on buildings, keep the PNG path as an optional
export for hero art only, and decide the team-colour question before any model is redrawn.
