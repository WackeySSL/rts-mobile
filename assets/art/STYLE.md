## Look
**Since ADR 0023 (Michael 2026-10-26) everything on the map is drawn in the isometric 2:1 view, flat
shaded with three tones per face and one light from the north-west; see "Projection", "Light" and
"Generated art" right below this section.** The rest of this section is the look of ADR 0022 and
stays until the art rows P101 to P105 replace the art it describes.

Realistic and muted, readable at small size on a phone (ADR 0022, Michael 2026-10-26: the first look
was "too square and too toy-like", he wants it "more true to nature"). Real materials (poured
concrete, corrugated steel, tarmac, sandbags, canvas, rust, dirt), believable proportions and detail
density, soft shading and weathering, small team markings. Not cartoonish, not photoreal, no flat
colour fields. One light from the top left for everything. **Buildings and units are generated
isometric models** since P103, P104, P101 and P102 (the eight buildings of each faction, see "Buildings",
and the six units of each, see "Derrick League units" and "Lumen Accord units"). The ore cells
keep their flat look, so the rules below that speak of the 3 px outline or of flat colours still
describe them; the unit icons, the tab icons and the glyphs stay flat, and the five hand-drawn
building item icons keep the oblique view they were drawn in until P114 moves their readers (see
"Icons and glyphs").

## Projection
ADR 0023 section 1; the canvas's Spec 01. The sim keeps its square grid; only the drawing is
projected, and the map is never rotated.
- A ground point (gx, gy) in tiles and a height z in px above it is drawn at
  `((gx - gy) x 32, (gx + gy) x 16 - z)` px at zoom 1. A tile is a 64 x 32 diamond; +gx runs down
  and to the right on the screen, +gy down and to the left.
- **Author at 2x**: one tile is 128 x 64 px in the SVG, the match-start bake rasterises at
  `bake_scale` and the gallery at 0.5 x zoom. The generator's models are in tiles in all three
  axes: a cube of one tile is 39.2 px high at 1x (Spec 01 says 39), `Z_PX` = 78.38 px per tile at
  2x. The camera looks from +gx, +gy and 30 degrees up.
- **Directions**: `d00` to `d15` in 22.5 degree steps. `d_k` faces the grid angle `(k - 6) x 22.5`
  degrees from +gx towards +gy: `d06` faces +gx (south-east on the screen, the Derrick League's
  default view), `d10` +gy (south-west, the Lumen Accord's), `d00` up the screen, `d08` down it.
  Vehicles, turrets and a defence's head have 16; a squad has the even ones (8); a building has one
  view and no direction in its file name.
- **Anchors** (Spec 03): a unit's frame is anchored at its ground point; a building's at the back
  corner of its footprint's diamond, 32 x n px above the footprint's centre at 2x for an n x n
  footprint. Every footprint is square. A building's canvas is exactly the footprint wide (128 x n
  at 2x) and as tall as its model needs above the back corner and the diamond's front corner below.
- **A part's frames share one canvas and one anchor**, the union over all of its directions plus a
  2 px margin, so an atlas sheet needs one frame box per part. A turret or head part has its own
  origin at its pivot; the pivot's place in the hull (`ArtModel.mount`) turns with the hull, and
  `ArtGen.mount_px` gives it in px for each direction (the sheets carry it, `IsoSheetDef.mounts`).

## Light
ADR 0023 section 5; the canvas's Spec 02. One light for the whole game, from the north-west.
- **Three tones per face**: top 100 %, left face 82 %, right face 62 % of the top tone. The light
  is a plane in the world, so a face keeps its tone when the model turns: a face's tone comes from
  its normal in the world, `0.72 + 0.28 nz - 0.10 nx + 0.10 ny` cut at 0.915 and 0.70, which gives
  the three values for the normals up, +gy and +gx; a slope towards the south-east is a left-face
  tone, one towards the north-west is lit. There are no gradients in a generated frame.
- **A material is one colour, its mid tone** (the left face); the top is that colour divided by
  0.82 (white where it clamps), the right face 0.62 / 0.82 of it. A **team zone** uses the
  faction's key triad as it is: light on the top, mid on the left face, dark on the right face.
- **Edges** are 2 px (not a detail thinner than 2 px at 2x): a dark shade of the face's own
  material (0.45 of its mid tone) on the sides, a thin light edge (the top tone 40 % towards white)
  on top faces, so every top edge catches the light (Spec 02). A team zone's edges are its key
  colours. A sphere is three discs, dark, mid and a lit one up and left, in a ring of 16 points.
  A `rod` has no outline (generator v5b, below).
- **Cast shadows** are separate frames (`<id>_shadow_d<NN>.svg`, a building's `<id>_shadow.svg`, one
  per hull frame): every primitive is dropped onto the ground towards **+gx, the south-east of the
  screen** (Spec 02), a point `0.8 x its height` tiles further along +gx (`ArtShadow.REACH`), and
  a convex solid's shadow is the hull of its dropped corners, a sphere's an ellipse (a `rod`
  casts none, generator v5b below). Black, one
  polygon per primitive, **35 % as one group** (`<g opacity="0.35">`, so where two overlap the
  shadow is no darker; the group is why a shadow file has a decimal that is an opacity). The light
  does not turn with a unit: the unit is turned first, so every frame's shadow falls to the right.
  The shadow holds the hull and the turret or head at its mount, straight ahead; a turret that
  turns away keeps the hull's shadow (the turret's own is small). The contact shadow under a
  volume is the base of the same polygon. The shadow frame has its own canvas, the union over the
  directions, and the hull's anchor; a building's is anchored at the same back corner (its canvas
  is at least the footprint wide, the shadow may reach past the east corner). No shadow inside a
  hull, turret or building frame. The view draws them at z 2 for units and in the `DecalLayer`
  (z 1) for buildings (ADR 0023 section 2). **Shadow frames bake onto the atlas's second page**
  (P237, ADR 0036 section 15), not the sorted world's; P204's key colour in them makes per-player
  slots there, which `TODAY2_*` in `test_iso_atlas_budget.gd` pins.
- **Occlusion pad and contact line** (P203, ADR 0035 section 4): every generated shadow frame holds,
  under the cast shadow and in the same `<g opacity="0.35">`, a pad and a contact line.
  - **Pad:** `AO_STEPS` (3) stepped rings of the ground outline (the hull of the plan of every
    primitive that starts below `GROUND_Z`, 0.25 tiles, so it also lies under a nose or a rifle),
    each pushed out a third more, to `ArtShadow.AO_REACH` (0.3 tiles), each a black fill at
    `fill-opacity="0.3"`, so the rings stack darker towards the base. No gradient.
  - **Contact line:** a `stroke-width="2"` round-joined black stroke, opaque in the group, round
    only what stands on the ground (primitives that start below `CONTACT_Z`, 0.02 tiles), so it
    runs where the model touches the ground and not under an overhang. A squad has one line per
    figure (the group's first letter, a, b, c), not one round the bare ground between them; a
    squad's pad stays one outline round all of them. Darkest at the contact line.
  - The cast shadow lies over both (opaque in the group), so the pad shows only where no shadow
    falls: the west, north and south of the base. No ink goes into a hull, turret or building frame,
    and the item cards keep their own shadow.
  - **The wellhead** is a prop with a 140 x 72 canvas and has a deliberately short hand-drawn pad:
    rings at 103, 106 and 109 % of its base diamond (about 0.04 tiles), and the contact line is the
    base diamond itself. The canvas has no room for 0.3 tiles and the slab's own shadow hides most of
    it; it is not the generated pad.
  - Page 2 grew from 1116, 570, 634 to 1514, 704, 814 rows (cross-faction, Derrick League mirror,
    Lumen Accord mirror), within 3584.
- **Team colour** is 4 to 17 % of the pixels of a default view in the blockouts (`tests/art` pins
  3 to 20 %); the art rows aim at 8 to 12 %: bands, cranes, stripes, light strips and lamps seen
  from above, never a large plate. A band is a slab slightly larger than the wall it wraps and
  stands between two wall segments, so no roof is a team plate: a turret has a ring between two
  segments, a cab a band, a soldier a chest band, never a disc or a cap on top. `tests/art` pins
  it (`test_artgen_plates.gd`): a team primitive is a roof plate when it is flat, its top face is
  open (less than a quarter of it has anything on it) and it covers 30 % of the top it rests on or
  is larger than 0.2 square tiles; a band, a stripe across a deck, an upright lintel and a ring
  round a barrel or a drum are not.

## Generated art
ADR 0023 section 5; plan rows P99 (the generator and the blockouts) and P99b (shadows, sheets,
cards, the ellipse). Run `bash scripts/gen-art.sh [--all | --model PATH] [--check]`: with no
argument (or `--all`) it regenerates every model and writes only the files that differ, so a second
run writes nothing; `--model PATH` does one model (`res://...`, a path from the project root or an
absolute one) and the index; `--check` writes nothing, names the files that differ (the first 20)
and exits 4. Exit 0 done (also when nothing changed, or `--check` found nothing), 1 an invalid
model or a script error, 2 a wrong argument or a model file that is not there, 3 no Godot, 4
`--check` found a difference. Never edit a generated file, change its model and generate again. The
generator is GDScript in `tools/artgen/` and runs in the pinned Godot headless.
- **A model** is `assets/art/models/<faction>/<type_id>.tres`, an `ArtModel`: `art_id`, `faction`
  (`fossil` or `renew`), `category` (`veh`, `inf`, `bld`), `footprint` (buildings), `silhouette`
  (one line in words, written before the model), `mount` (the turret or head pivot) and `prims`,
  one primitive per entry: `<shape> <part> <material> <cx> <cy> <z0> <sx> <sy> <sz> [options]`.
  Lengths are tiles; a unit faces +x and stands on its origin; a building's origin is its
  footprint's centre. Shapes: `box`; `wedge` (a box whose top slopes down towards +x, `slope=` 0
  to 1, a negative one slopes towards -x; 1 comes down to the bottom of the box); `prism` (`sides=` 3 to 24, default 12, `taper=` scales the top, `axis=x|y|z` lays it
  along its length; the size is the width across the flats, a hexagon is 1.155 times as long on
  its second axis); `sphere` (diameter `sx`). Parts: `hull`, `turret`, `head`. Material: a name of
  the faction's palette below, or `team`. The `shadow` part is never authored: it is made from the
  rest. **The muzzle** (for `muzzle_lift_px`) is not authored either: it is the centre height of
  the primitive of the firing part (the turret or head, a squad's or a gun-less vehicle's hull)
  that reaches farthest along +x, so the barrel must be the primitive that reaches farthest forward.
- **Painter's order**: back to front by the camera, a primitive before another when their boxes
  are apart along an axis and the other is on the camera's side. So keep primitives apart along an
  axis, touching is fine: split a drum into segments and put the band between them, put a
  turret's barrel against the face it comes out of.
- **Wedge faces** (P99c). `ArtMesh` turns each face's normal away from the mean of the solid's
  corners, which lies inside any convex solid. P99 turned it away from the middle of the
  primitive's box, and a wedge with `slope=1` has its sloped face through that middle: the dot
  product was zero, rounding noise picked the sign, and the renderer culled the face when it went
  the wrong way (a `slope=1` hood at the Flarejack's place did). So `slope=1` (and `-1`, and a
  `box` with a `slope=`) is safe at any position. The models that stood in for it with `slope=0.95` moved to
  `slope=1`: the Flarejack's hood, the Bullhead's glacis, the Mortar Rig's cab, the Swift's nose,
  the glass fronts of the Arcline and the Fuel Runner, the Grid Hub's two and the Crew Hall's three
  solar panels (11 wedges in 8 models; 198 files regenerated: their frames, shadows and item
  cards). The design slopes (0.9, 0.85, 0.8, 0.6, 0.5 and the Heavy Yard's -1) are as they were.
  `tests/art/test_artgen_wedge.gd` pins the sloped face at 10 positions, its normal against a point
  inside, its drawing at all 16 yaws and that no model uses `slope=0.95`.
- **Files**: `assets/art/iso/<faction>/<category>/<type_id>_<part>_d<NN>.svg` (`d<NN>` left out for
  a building body), parts `hull`, `turret`, `head`, a building's damage patches `dmg1` and `dmg2`
  (`<type_id>_dmg1.svg`, one frame, no direction; "Damage poses" below) and `shadow`, each with a
  "Keep File" `.import`
  (`importer="keep"`): the export carries the SVG text and the bake reads it. A frame is one comment
  line (the model it came from), the root with the canvas, one `g` with the 2 px edge and one
  `polygon` per face; hex colours in upper case, coordinates with one decimal. At most 28 KB (the
  blockouts are under 14 KB). Beside them `<type_id>_sheet.tres` (below), in `assets/art/iso/` the
  `index.tres` and in `assets/art/iso/icons/` the item cards `item_<type_id>.svg` (one folder for
  both factions, so a build tile finds its card by type id; the 12 hand-drawn `item_<id>.svg` in
  `svg/icons/` stay until P114 moves their readers). 178 shadow frames, 28 sheets, 28 cards.
- **Tight boxes** (P305, ADR 0036 section 15): `IsoSheetDef.boxes` gives, for every frame of a part with 16 or 8 directions except the shadows (vehicle and squad hulls, walk and idle, turrets, heads), the drawn box plus 1 clear px in the part's canvas: the polygon bounds plus `ArtGen.MARGIN_PX` (2), of which the 2 px edge stroke takes 1. `frame_box(part, dir, frame)` reads it; the whole canvas for a part without boxes (one view, a shadow). Shadows stay untrimmed: they are on page 2, which has room. The frames are unchanged; P306 packs the boxes (the texel formula is in `IsoSheetDef.boxes`' comment).
- **Sheets** (`IsoSheetDef`, a `Resource` in `tools/artgen/`; `IsoIndex` holds all of them, sorted
  by art id, `IsoIndex.sheet(art_id)`): per part the frame count (16, 8 or 1), the canvas and the
  anchor, in px of the 2x art (a unit's anchor is its ground point, a building's body and shadow
  the footprint's back corner, `origin(part)` gives the footprint's centre, and so for a damage
  patch; a turret's or head's anchor is its pivot); `mounts`, the pivot of the turret or head from
  the hull's ground point for each hull frame (`mount(dir)`; one for a building's head, none without a turret); the three
  lifts in **world px at zoom 1**, which is what the view counts in: `bar_lift_px` how high the
  picture reaches above the ground point in the pose that three of four hull frames stay under (the
  turret or head straight ahead; a bar standing on it clears those poses and covers only a taller
  one, a long hull or a barrel that points up the screen, which "Health bars" allows),
  `pick_lift_px` half of that (a tap picks a unit round its lifted middle), `muzzle_lift_px` the
  height of the muzzle (0 for a building without a head); and the card's path. The sheets are
  written by `ArtSheet` as `.tres` text by hand (Godot cannot save a resource to a string), so
  they are bytes like the frames and `tests/art` compares them with a fresh run. The per-match
  bake (P113) loads `index.tres` and reads each frame at `sheet.path(part, dir)` (`sheet.path(part,
  dir, frame)` with the frame axis, next bullet).
- **The frame axis** (P134, ADR 0027 section 4; the four squads have poses since P139, "Squad poses"
  below, and "Building life (P143)" for the buildings' `anim` parts).
  The sets (ADR 0027): a squad gets `walk` (4 frames) and `idle` (2) in its 8 directions, a
  building's `anim` has at most 16 frames, cropped to the moving element; shadows are not posed (the
  standing shadow serves every pose). A primitive may carry `group=<name>` (hull and `anim`
  primitives only); its part may be `anim`, a building's moving element, drawn only by the `anim`
  frames (not by `hull`) and at rest in the shadow and the card. The `anim` sprite draws over the
  whole body, so no hull part can hide the element: model it where nothing stands in front. A
  model's `poses`: `<part> <frames>` (`walk`, `idle` or `anim`, 2 to 16 frames per direction) and
  one move a line, `<part> <frame> <group> shift <dx> <dy> <dz>` or `... turn <x|y|z> <degrees> <px>
  <py> <pz>` (tiles; a turn goes about the axis through the pivot, positive as the hull's yaw: x
  towards y about z, y towards z about x, z towards x about y; moves apply in line order). `walk`
  and `idle` pose a squad's `hull`, `anim` the `anim` primitives; they have the hull's directions (a
  building's `anim` none). Files `<id>_<part>_d<NN>_f<K>.svg` (K from 0), a building's
  `<id>_anim_f<K>.svg`; a part of one frame has no `_f<K>`. A pose part has one canvas, anchored at
  the ground point (an `anim` at the footprint centre); the bar lift clears the `anim` element in
  its highest frame. Model fields `stride_milli` (needs `walk`), `anim_ms` (needs `anim`),
  `emitters` (`<kind> <x> <y> <z>` in tiles; `smoke`, `steam`, `flame`, `exhaust`, `launch`,
  `damage`, `dmg_gas`, `dmg_spark` and `hang`, an ambient one with `group=<name>`; see "Damage
  poses") and
  `marks` (`tracks` or `wheels`, vehicles). The sheet gains `frames` (`frame_count(part)` says 1
  when absent), `stride_milli`, `anim_ms`, `emitters` with `emitter_points` (px of the 2x art, per
  hull frame for each, once for a building: `emitter_point(index, dir)`) and `marks`, each written
  only when set, so today's sheets are unchanged. `tests/art/test_artgen_frames.gd` pins it.
- **Damage poses** (P156, ADR 0028 sections 3 and 4; generator v4, extended by v5a, P163, ADR
  0029 section 5; since P160 the eight vehicles carry `damage`, `hang` and `scar_milli`; the Derrick
  League's eight buildings use the damage poses since P158a, "Damage stages" below, and the Lumen
  Accord's since P158b). A building's `poses` gain `dmg1` and `dmg2`, its body at
  damage stage 1 and 2, a move a line, frame
  0 and no header line: `dmg1|dmg2 0 <group> shift <dx> <dy> <dz>`, `... turn <x|y|z> <degrees>
  <px> <py> <pz>`, `... hide` (the group is not drawn) or `... char <percent>` (0 to 100: its
  face colours, edges too, are mixed that far towards the char colour **#2A2522**; a charred `team`
  primitive leaves the key band or the generator refuses the model: the Derrick League's does from
  about 97 %, the Lumen Accord's from about 86 %, where its mid key is a blue-grey, #283739, not the
  char colour; a charred face is no longer a key colour, so the bake does not recolour it). The groups are the
  hull's. A hull primitive tagged `damage=K` (1 or 2) is drawn from stage K on and never in the
  intact body, the shadow or the card. **Stage 2 does not inherit stage 1's moves** (list them
  again), but the
  `damage=1` primitives do appear at stage 2. A model has both stages or stage 2 alone. The group
  `mount` (a defence's Head seat) and the groups of the `anim` primitives may not be touched by a
  damage move: the generator fails such a model, so the moving element stays seated; the `anim`
  part runs at stage 1 and follows `anim_kritisk` at stage 2. (There is no ruin picture: ADR 0029's ruin was dropped on
  2026-11-04, a collapse ends in the crater.)
  **The patch.** Each stage is a part of its own, `<type_id>_dmg1.svg` and `_dmg2.svg`, drawn with
  today's projection, light, edge and team key. The stage is drawn in its own greedy painter's
  order (the depth between the changed and the untouched primitives is the stage's). Taking away or
  moving one primitive can draw two untouched ones in the other order than the intact body, so the
  cut also covers where such a pair overlaps on the screen (the intersection of their outlines, not
  their union: the patch grows only where the swap shows). The cut is read off the geometry, not by
  comparing pixels: `cuts` (in the sheet, `[stage 1, stage 2]`, body canvas
  rows) is **the first intact row**, the lowest screen row of the outlines of what the stage changes
  (moved, turned, hidden, charred or added primitives, intact and posed) and of the swapped
  overlaps, plus half the 2 px edge, rounded up; from that row down the stage picture equals the
  intact one. The patch is the stage picture from its top down to the cut plus a 4 px overlap, cropped left and right to its ink, with
  the body's anchor rule (the back corner, `origin(part)` the footprint's centre). The view shows the
  body from the cut down and the patch over it; in the four overlap rows an edge is drawn by both,
  which doubles its antialiased fringe (up to about 80 of 255, edge pixels only). The generator
  refuses a change that is wholly above the body's canvas or reaches the foot of the body (the
  overlap would not fit), and a stage that changes nothing; its message names the primitive that
  sets the lowest row, with its line in the model and the pose lines that move it. The art rule is
  that damage sits in the top third of a body; the page test, not a percentage, is the arbiter.
  **Sheet and model fields.** `cuts`; `scar_milli` (a vehicle's scar scale, 1000 for the battle
  tank, vehicles only); the emitter kinds `damage` (where a scar sits and damage smoke starts; the
  counts are under "Generator v5a" below) and `hang` (a vehicle's bracket, one at most, none on a
  building; the generator mirrors the point across the hull's long axis in every hull frame so it
  is on the side that faces the camera); and `IsoSheetDef.root`, empty for every generated sheet
  (the frames are in `assets/art/iso/<faction>/<category>/`), set only by the hand-written neutral
  sheet of the damage overlays (P160), where it is the folder its frames are in.
  `tests/art/test_artgen_damage.gd` and `test_artgen_damage_rules.gd` pin it on crafted models.
  **Generator v5a** (P163, ADR 0029 section 5), all of it on crafted models in
  `tests/art/test_artgen_v5_materials.gd` and `test_artgen_v5_points.gd`:
  - **Materials**, the same in both factions and outside both key bands: `interior` (the dark
    room, two flat tones, **#1A1715** on top and left faces, **#100E0D** on right faces and edges,
    no light ramp), `slab` (a slab's edge, mid **#8F8A7F**), `hole` (a blown pane, near-black
    blue-grey, mid **#20262B**), `rebar` (rust-dark, mid **#4A4038**) and `beam` (steel, mid
    **#5C645B**); the ramp is the usual top 122 %, left 100 %, right 76 % of the mid tone.
  - **Moves.** `<stage> 0 <group> blow <percent>` turns the first `roundi(n x percent / 100)` of
    the group's intact `glass` primitives, in the hash order (djb2 over the art id's UTF-8 bytes,
    mixed with the line index by an integer `_mix`; no randomness), into `hole`; the frames, other primitives of the group, stay. A
    `blow` on a group with no intact glass is refused.
  - **Points.** A building has the typed damage points `damage` (fire and smoke), `dmg_gas` (a gas
    jet, a fire at the torn opening) and `dmg_spark` (sparks, no fire); a vehicle has
    one `damage` and one `hang`, infantry none. A building with damage poses has **2 to 4** typed
    points together. A building without damage poses keeps the v4 limit of two points, and only
    `damage` ones (`dmg_gas` and `dmg_spark` are refused there). The ink tests below run only for a
    building with damage poses. The ink test is geometric, no
    raster: a damage point lies inside a drawn face of stage 1 (the intact picture plus any
    `damage=1` primitive when there is no `dmg1`) and of stage 2, and above `dmg1`'s cut row. The
    tests wait for a sound cut.
  - **Ambient emitters that break.** `smoke`, `steam` and `flame` may end in `group=<name>` (a
    hull group; other kinds refuse it). The sheet's `emitter_quiet` (parallel to `emitters`; written
    only when one is not 0) holds the first stage, 1 or 2, whose pose shifts, turns or hides the
    group, 0 for never or no group. For a building with damage poses the emitter needs ink in the
    intact picture and at each stage before its quiet stage.
  - **`anim_kritisk`** (model field, `run` or `hide`) is required of a building with an `anim`
    and a `dmg2`, and written as the sheet's bool `anim_kritisk` (true for `run`). `run` is refused
    when the lowest vertex of the `anim` element over all its frames has no ink under it in `dmg2`.
  - **Canvas.** The seam of the trimmed body (ADR 0029 section 11) holds only for even canvas
    heights; `ArtGen` rounds every canvas up to even, so the refusal of an odd body canvas is a
    guard against a later change, not a rule a model can break today.
  - **Key share.** `tests/art/artgen_v5_support.gd` rasterises a stage (`Image.load_svg_from_string`)
    and measures its key-band pixels against the intact picture's: SKADET 55 to 85 %, KRITISK 20 to
    50 %; P158a and P158b run it on the real buildings.
  **Generator v5b** (P163b, ADR 0029 section 5), on crafted models in `tests/art/test_artgen_v5b.gd`;
  a model that uses none of it gives the same bytes as before:
  - **`broken <part> <material> cx cy z0 sx sy sz teeth=N low=f`** is a box whose top edge, along
    its longer horizontal axis, is a zigzag of `N` points (2 to 12, default 5) between `f` (above
    0, below 1, default 0.5) and the full height, high and low in turn. The heights come from an integer hash
    of the art id and the line (the inlined djb2 and `_mix` of `blow`; no randomness), so two runs
    agree and two lines differ. Faces: front and back with the zigzag, two ends, the floor and one
    quad per tooth segment. It is one concave primitive: its tops are drawn far to near, then the
    front and the end cap over them, then the light edge of each visible tooth's front segment (no
    slices, so no seams), and each tooth quad is oriented
    against a point below its own middle.
  - **`rod <part> <material> x0 y0 z0 x1 y1 z1 width [bend=deg]`** is one polygon quad `width` art
    px across (at least 2) in the material's mid tone, with no outline, ordered at its midpoint's
    depth. `bend` (at most 90 degrees) turns the half towards the second point about the
    horizontal axis (up x direction from the first point to the second), right-handed, and makes
    two quads: a rod written bottom to top swings its far half towards +x, one written top to
    bottom towards -x, and a rod written along +x droops. It takes any material. "No outline" is ADR
    0029 section 5's decision; "casts no shadow" is the generator's own (a rod has no faces to
    drop; the Edges and Cast shadows rules above do not apply to it).
  - **`petals <part> <material> cx cy z diameter n tilt [group=] [damage=]`** expands when the
    model is read into `n` (3 to 16) thin plates of the material round the opening, each turned
    outward by `tilt` degrees (above 0, at most 90) about its base, and an `interior` disc 0.03
    tile tall in the opening: `n` plates and one disc, in exact counts, all in the line's group.
    Only in `hull` and `anim` (a turret or head would shift the plates' pivots). The primitives
    of one line have keys of their own for `blow` and the swap test; a message names the `petals`
    line.
  - There is no `rubble` helper: a KRITISK patch cannot reach the foot, so a ground heap has no
    stage to be in.
- **Item cards** (`ArtIcon`): 128 x 128 px, a dark plate (#1C2127, rim #2F363D, the corners cut), a
  faint 2:1 grid (#2A3036, lines every 16 px through the centre, so the diamonds are 32 x 16), the
  type's default view (`d06`, `d10` for the Lumen Accord, a building's one view, a turret or head
  straight ahead) with its cast shadow, scaled to fit 104 x 92 px (at most 1.5 x the 2x art, so a
  squad is not lost), and a corner in the faction's key mid colour. Polygons only; the colours of
  the plate are greys (saturation under 0.30), so the key band holds. A card is a texture import
  (Godot writes its `.import`, `svg/scale=1.0`), not "Keep File": it is drawn at 64 dp in a build
  tile and is not baked, so it keeps its faction's own triad.
- **Tests** (`tests/art/test_artgen*.gd`): two runs give identical bytes, every committed frame,
  sheet, card and the index equals a fresh run and none is left over, the directions are complete,
  only `svg`, `g` and `polygon` are used, every part has one canvas, a building is anchored at its
  back corner, the key band holds, every type has a team share of 3 to 20 % in its default view and
  no team primitive is a roof plate, and each default view has a body and a spread of tones at 32
  px in greyscale. `test_artgen_shadows.gd` pins the drop (+gx, 0.8 per tile of height, the same
  way at every yaw, a sphere's ellipse, one group at 35 %), `test_artgen_sheets.gd` each sheet

  `test_artgen_cards.gd` the cards and the ellipse, `test_gen_art_cli.gd` the exit codes of
  `scripts/gen-art.sh` and `tools/artgen/gen_art.gd` (`--check` of a model that is not committed
  is exit 4).
- **The key band** (ADR 0023 section 5): a colour other than the faction's three key colours must
  not have an HSV hue within 20 degrees of a key colour's hue and a saturation of at least 0.30.
  The Derrick League's band is 1.6 to 45.3 degrees, the Lumen Accord's 170.3 to 213.0. Greys and

### Squad poses (P139)
Plan row P139 gives each of the four squads (Rig Hands, Wildcatters, Linemen, Breakers) the walk
and the idle that Michael asked for: soldiers move their legs when they walk and fidget a little
when they stand (ADR 0027 sections 4 and 5). The standing figures are not touched: every hull
frame, shadow, card and canvas is as it was, and a pose is a model pose, never an edited frame.
The silhouette of a pose is the standing one with the legs scissored (walk) or the head turned and
the weight shifted (idle): the same three figures in the same places, the same hats, rifles, tubes
and team bands.
- **The model.** Every primitive of a squad's hull carries a group `<figure>_<limb>`: figures `a`, `b`
  and `c` in the order of the file, limbs `legl`, `legr` (the two legs, a box each), `body` (torso,
  band, arms, weapon and pack) and `head` (neck, helmet, lamp or visor). `walk` has 4 frames and
  `idle` 2 (`poses`), each with 8 directions, `d00` to `d14` in steps of two, in `<id>_walk_d<NN>_f<K>.svg`
  and `<id>_idle_d<NN>_f<K>.svg`: 48 frames a squad, 192 in all. A leg turns about the hip (the top
  of the leg, `turn y`; a positive angle swings the foot back); the body and head shift in z so
  that the lowest planted foot stands on the ground (a tilted leg's corner would sink or float),
  which is the small bob of a step.
- **Walk, 4 frames.** Contact: one foot 36 degrees forward, the other 36 back, the body at its
  lowest. Passing: the planted leg 10 degrees back and the swinging leg 10 forward and 0.035 tile
  up, the body at its highest. Then the same with the feet swapped. A figure's four frames are four
  different steps.
- **A frame apart.** The three figures of a squad are never in the same step: figure `i` does in
  frame `f` what figure `a` does in frame `f + i` (modulo the frame count, 4 for a walk and 2 for
  an idle), so a squad does not march in lockstep (`test_artgen_poses.gd` pins it move for move).
- **Idle, 2 frames.** The head turns 25 degrees (30 for the hexagonal Lumen Accord helmets) to one
  side and the body sways 0.03 tile to it, while the opposite foot steps 10 degrees forward (a
  `turn y`, set on the ground); the second frame does the other side. Both frames differ from the
  standing frame, so a squad that fidgets always shows a change. Two frames for three figures
  leave `a` and `c` in the same pose, so `c` looks the other way (its head's angle is the opposite;
  the sway and the foot are `a`'s). No arm moves: the weapon is held across the chest and moves
  with the body.
- **`stride_milli`** is the ground travel of one walk cycle in milli-tiles (the frame advances every
  quarter of it, ADR 0027 section 5): 352 for the Rig Hands and the Wildcatters, 376 for the
  Linemen and the Breakers. A figure's leg is 0.15 tile (0.16 for the Lumen Accord) long, so a 36
  degree swing carries its foot 2 x 0.15 x sin 36 = 0.176 tile back over a half cycle, which is
  what half of 352 asks (and 0.188 for 376): the planted foot does not slide (4 x leg x sin 36
  is the stride, a multiple of 4 because the view floors a quarter of it, a test pins it). At their
  speeds (67, 60, 73 and 60 milli-tiles a tick, 15 ticks a second) the squads walk 2.9, 2.6, 2.9 and
  2.4 cycles a second; `test_artgen_poses.gd` holds every squad of `data/units` to 1.5 to 3.5.
- **Light.** `ArtRender`'s fill cuts were chosen for box faces at 16 yaws. A face turned by a pose can
  land on one and band from frame to frame, so the angles are in the windows that keep every
  visible turned face at least 0.016 of shade from a cut: 9 to 11 and 35 to 38 degrees about the
  hip, 25 degrees about z for an octagonal or boxy head, 30 for a hexagonal one (a hexagon's best,
  as far from the cut as the standing helmets). `test_artgen_poses.gd` checks every face of every
  turned primitive in every frame and direction. No new colour: a pose frame uses the faction's
  materials, its triad and their edges, and the key band holds.
- **Order and edges.** A pose frame is drawn in the order of the standing figure and its light
  edges are the standing figure's top faces (`ArtRender.render` takes the standing primitives, `rest`,
  from `ArtModel.frame_rest`): a leg that swings or lifts rises into the boxes of its own torso,
  where the centre-depth fallback would draw it over the waist, the pack or the rifle (a dark notch
  that came and went), and a leg swung 36 degrees has a side face that faces 0.59 up, which is not a
  roof and gets no light edge (a grey blink on the dark legs in every contact frame). The fill still
  follows the posed face, so a tilted face is a little lighter. An `anim` element turns all round
  and is judged as posed. `test_artgen_poses.gd` pins the order and the edges of all 192 frames.
  **The trade-off:** the standing order lets a turned head's helmet hide part of its own visor or
  lamp in some idle frames (`d04` and `d12`; in review the Breakers' idle `d12` f0, figure `b`, showed
  4.2 px of its visor against 19.8 true, at 2x). An order that follows the pose for some groups was
  worse on the walk legs, so the standing order stays. **`REST_ORDER` (in `ArtModel`) is a whole
  pose part's:** a pose that moves a part in front of the body, as P154's firing pose raising the
  rifle will, must not simply join it (the rifle would be drawn where it is not); it needs a part or
  a group order of its own.
- **Cost.** A pose canvas is the union of its frames and directions, at most 4 px wider or taller
  than the hull's (walk and idle: 86 x 66 and 86 x 66 for the Rig Hands, 104 x 76 and 108 x 76
  for the Wildcatters, 90 x 72 for the Linemen, 116 x 78 and 120 x 78 for the Breakers). The sorted
  world's page at bake scale 1.0 grows from 2682 to 3066 px for the cross-faction match (budget
  3584), from 2206 to 2524 for a Derrick League mirror and from 2226 to 2624 for a Lumen Accord
  mirror (`tests/view/test_iso_atlas_budget.gd` pins them); the 192 files are 2.4 MiB of SVG, at
  most 16.6 KB each.
- **The gallery's Units page** plays them: each squad of the four lines stands for a second, walks
  2.4 s, stands, shows idle 0 for 1.3 s, stands, shows idle 1 for 1.3 s (an idle window, the
  standing frame, the other idle frame, as the game will), the squad made next 0.9 s further on in

- **Readability.** At 32 px in greyscale every pose frame keeps a body and a spread of tones

- **Originality.** The poses are plain real-world ones: a stride and a glance. No salute, no

## Scale
- 1 tile = 64 px in game at zoom 1. Author at 2x: 1 tile = 128 px in the SVG.
- Canvas at 2x: an ore cell 128x128, a rally flag 128x128. (The top-down units' 64x64, 96x96,
  128x128 and 160x160 are gone: a generated unit's canvas is the union of its frames, see
  "Derrick League units" and `IsoSheetDef.sizes`.) **A building's canvas is exactly the footprint
  wide** (128 px per footprint tile, so 128 x n for an n x n footprint) and as tall as its model
  needs, in the isometric view, anchored at the footprint's back corner (P103; see "Projection" and
  "Buildings"). **Icons and glyphs are 128x128 with import scale 1.0 (new)**, because they are drawn
  at 24 to 64 dp, not in tiles (see "Icons and glyphs").

  The gallery's Units page shows scale 1; its zoom row shows the Flarejack at 0.75, 1.0 and 1.5, drawn
  at the sharpness each zoom asks for, as the game's atlas does.

a team layer has R = G = B, so the runtime tint stays clean.

## Drawing rules
- **No heavy outline (new, ADR 0022).** A thin dark edge, about 1.5 px at 2x, in a dark shade of the
  material on the silhouette and the inner details; a soft dark contact rim where a volume meets the
  ground; a short soft shadow. Icons keep an edge of 3 to 4 px in #1C2127 (they are seen small on the
  dark HUD tile), and the art that v1 drew with the 3 px outline (ore, flags, effects) keeps it
  until P105 and P98 redraw it.
- Three tones per material plus a highlight on edges that face the top left. Texture comes from
  gradients and dash arrays, never from a `pattern` (see "Technical rules").
- Filled shapes and gaps are at least 2 px at 2x. Edges and thin inner lines may be 1.5 px. (This is
  how we read the skill's "1.5 px inner details" together with "nothing thinner than 2 px".)
- **Elevation reads as lightness.** The lower the plane, the darker the olive: hull deck #4E5A3A,
  turret #6B7A4F, hatches and barrel in metal, highlights #8C9A6B / #A9B4BE. This keeps the turret
  apart from the hull at 32 px greyscale without a bigger palette.
- **Buildings (P103).** Generated in the isometric view with the one light (see "Light"): the roof
  lit, the left wall mid, the right wall darkest, and a cast shadow towards the south-east as a frame
  of its own that reaches past the footprint. A building's rotating part (the Redoubt's head) has its
  own 16 frames and casts no shadow of its own: the building's shadow holds it.
- The silhouette must be identifiable at 32 px in greyscale. A gun or other weapon must also stick
  out of the body as its own shape in the pure silhouette, at least 3 px long and 3 px thick at
  32 px (see "Readability check").
- **Team colour covers about 8 to 12 % of the visible surface (new, ADR 0022 question 3, Michael:
  ja)**, as marks that are seen from above: a stripe along an eave, a cornice or a ridge, a band round a
  wall or a cylinder, a lintel, a girder, a rail. Never a large plate, and a roof is never one. Ownership is therefore
  weaker at zoom 0.75 than the old 20 to 35 %; the gallery shows both teams at every zoom, so judge it
  there. The generated units have it since P101.

## Technical rules
- One drawing per file with `width`, `height` and `viewBox="0 0 W H"` set to the canvas.
- Allowed elements: `svg`, `g`, `path`, `rect`, `circle`, `ellipse`, `polygon`, `polyline`, `line`,
  `defs`, `linearGradient`, `radialGradient` and its `stop` children, `clipPath`, and `use` that
  references only `href="#id"` of an id in the same file (new, ADR 0022). A group `opacity` and dash
  arrays are fine and flatten overlapping shapes (used for the shadows and for corrugation). A `mask`
  only with solid fills: Godot 4.7.2's ThorVG hides everything under a gradient-filled mask. **No
  `pattern`**: ThorVG draws none, so the file would look empty in the game (the texture of a roof is a
  wide line with a short dash array, clipped or not). Not allowed: text, image, filter,
  foreignObject, external href, style blocks, scripts, raster data.
- At most 28 KB per file (new; was 20 KB); integer or one-decimal coordinates (gradient fractions and
  opacities may have two decimals). `tests/art/test_art_assets.gd` reads every file for these rules.
- Start each file with two XML comments: the layer, then the silhouette in one line that begins
  `Silhouette:` (`Shape:` for a team layer, `Pattern:` for a tile) and says what the shape is.
- Naming: `assets/art/svg/<category>/<name>[_hull|_turret|_team].svg` in snake_case.
- Godot imports SVG directly. Commit the `.svg.import` next to each file with `svg/scale=0.5`:
  1. Write the SVG, run `godot --headless --path . --import` (this creates the `.import` with
     `svg/scale=1.0`).
  2. Set `svg/scale=0.5` in the `.import` file and import again. The uid does not change.
  3. Check the texture: a 128 px canvas must load as 64x64 (`ui/selection_ring.svg` is the
     exception: import scale 1.0, 168 px).

## Layer contract (multi-layer flags)
Until P101 this was the contract of every multi-layer unit and until P103 of the oblique buildings;
units and buildings are generated models now (see "Generated art" and "Buildings"), and it holds for
the rally flags, which are drawn from two layers that share one canvas:

| Order | File | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `<name>_hull.svg` | The ground shadow, the foot plate, the pole and its finial |
| 2 | `<name>_team.svg` | White and grey team marks, tinted with `modulate` |

- Draw order across rigs, not per rig (ADR 0012 section 6, one z per batch group): every hull, then
  every team layer. Two overlapping rigs can therefore show a neighbour's team layer over this hull
  for a moment; the art must read right that way: every team mark keeps its own #4D4D4D edge, and
  no hull relies on its team layer to look finished.
- Hull and team share one orientation and one canvas, 128x128 at 2x.
- Import scale **0.5** for every layer, so 128 px becomes 64 px, one tile.
- Team layer: tinted at runtime with `modulate` (team A #2F80ED, team B #E5484D, neutral #9AA5B1).
- No layer turns any more (the **(new)** rule for rotating layers, no shadow and no one-sided
  light, went with the last one): a building's gun head, the Redoubt's, is a generated part of 16
  frames anchored at its own pivot, which the sheet's mount puts on the roof (see "Generated art"
  and "Buildings"), and a unit's turret is one too (see "Derrick League units").
- A unit or a building is not drawn this way: its team colour is in its SVG text (the faction's key
  triad, replaced by the player's at match start) and its frames are the generator's.

## Ore
Nine opaque tiles, `ore/ore_<level>[a|b].svg`, 128x128 at 2x, import scale 0.5, so one tile is
64 px. Each tile is dirt #8A6F4D to every edge, with speckles in the two new dirt tones and gold
nuggets in the ore colours (outline, highlight on the top left, shade on the lower right, shadow
offset 4,4). Nothing reaches the outer 4 px (2 px imported), so two tiles side by side show bare
dirt on both sides of the join and the map keeps its "no grid" rule.
- The level of a cell is 0 when `ore_amount` is 0 and `1 + (amount - 1) * 4 / ore_cell_max`
  (integer division) above it, so 1 to 5 is level 1, 6 to 10 level 2, 11 to 15 level 3 and 16 to 20
  level 4 at `ore_cell_max` 20. A cell that regrows climbs the levels one at a time.
- A tile name is its level, plus `a` or `b` from level 1 up: two layouts of the same gold, so a
  field does not repeat. The suggested pick is `(col + row) % 2`, so neighbours differ.

| File | Level | Look | Bright gold at 64 px |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ore_0.svg` | 0 | bare dirt, three dug-out pits with a lit lip, three dull flecks of gold | 0 |
| `ore_1a.svg`, `ore_1b.svg` | 1 | three small nuggets | 63 |
| `ore_2a.svg`, `ore_2b.svg` | 2 | one medium and three small | 137 |

There is no team layer: ore is neutral. The tiles are opaque, so they cover whatever colour

the id `ore` to `data/terrain/standard.tres`, and the same tree must carry the key `ore` in

terrain tables under "Fog and terrain" and the terrain lists of `tests/art`, because `tests/project`

keys) goes red without them. The proposal is the dirt the tiles are filled with: key `ore` #8A6F4D,
tile `0,0,8,8,ore`, probe 3,3. The key is not in this PR's tables because `MapView` is another
owner's file (see "Open points", the request for P70).
Greyscale check: the dirt (115) is as bright as grass (110 and 127), so the nuggets carry the field:
gold 180 to 212 with a 3 px outline at 32. At 32 px per tile in greyscale the five levels read apart

## Ghosts
A ghost is a building drawn as a plan: where the player is about to place one (ADR 0019 section 2,
ADR 0023 section 3), and a building last seen (ADR 0017 section 3, plan P84c, ADR 0023 section 2's
Fog and its decision 4, plan P115c). The two work differently. A **placement ghost** is a pattern
and a wash on the ground with the faded building over them. The one pattern tile is
`ui/ghost_hatch.svg`, seamless, white and grey only, tinted at run time like a team layer, 128x128
at 2x, import scale 0.5, so 64 px, drawn tiled (`TextureRect` with `stretch_mode` TILE, or a tiled
`draw_texture_rect`) over the **footprint's diamond**, because it also shows which cells are
blocked. Each placement meaning has its own shape (see "Shape as second cue"): bold diagonal bands
rising to the right mean it does not fit, and a solid frame and no pattern mean it fits. A
**last-seen ghost** has no pattern at all: it is the remembered building itself, drawn whole and
dimmed (below).

| Ghost | Building | Wash | Pattern | Frame |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fits | own team colour, the whole sprite, faded as one group | #30A46C on the footprint's diamond | none | solid round the diamond, #30A46C |
| Does not fit | own team colour, the whole sprite, faded as one group | #E5484D on the footprint's diamond | `ghost_hatch.svg` tinted #E5484D over the diamond | solid round the diamond, #E5484D |
| Last seen | the other side's colour, the whole body, not faded, dimmed as one layer (below) | none | none | none, no health bar, no ring |

A placement ghost is the building's sprite in the projection, anchored at the footprint's back corner
like a placed building (ADR 0023 section 3); the marks lie on the footprint's cells as diamonds (the
gallery draws one diamond for the whole footprint, the view one tile quad per cell). The draw order is
the wash on the diamond, the faded sprite, the hatch if it does not fit, the solid frame round the
diamond and the reason's glyph at its centre, so the cells that will be blocked read through the
sprite and what rises above them shows the faded sprite only. The gallery's ghost is the generated
frame of the barracks (`barracks_hull.svg`, Signal for the own ghost, Arktisk for the last seen one).

The numbers: sprite alpha 0.7 (the whole rig in one `CanvasGroup`, faded through its
`CanvasGroup.self_modulate`: `modulate` would reach the hull sprite too and blend twice, as
`MoveMarkerLayer` notes), wash alpha 0.3, hatch alpha 0.8 and a frame 3 dp wide (the marker stroke
rule, dp over zoom in world space; the gallery draws 3 base px). The reason's glyph for a placement
that does not fit is `icons/glyph_reason_<reason>.svg` (see "Icons and glyphs"). It is drawn on top
of the frame at 32 dp and tinted danger red like the hatch under it, so its file carries a dark seat
of its own: a glyph without one has the hatch's fill and the hatch's edge. The gallery centres
`glyph_reason_occupied` on the ghost that does not fit; the view of P86 places it.

**Last seen** (P115c, `game/view/GhostMemoryLayer.gd`; ADR 0023, Fog and decision 4): the ghost is
the remembered building's whole body, roof included, the same atlas region as the live building
and in the colour of its owner's side, drawn in one layer at **z 5**, over the fog on the ground
(z 4) and under every live object, so a unit that walks over a remembered building is drawn in
front of it. The whole layer carries **one dim colour** (`modulate`, the black of the explored fog
over the sprite: `1 - explored_alpha / 255` on each channel, 0.45 at the explored alpha of 140), so
a remembered building is as dark as the ground it stands on, every ghost is one draw call and no
sprite is tinted of its own. It has no dots, no pattern, no wash, no frame, no health bar, no ring
and no pick shape: a sprite and nothing else. It is drawn only where no cell of its footprint is
seen, so at any moment exactly one of the live building and its ghost is on the screen. ADR 0022's
last-seen ghost (the dots of `ghost_seen.svg` tinted #9AA5B1 over the sprite box, the base part
under the fog layer and the roof part dimmed by its quad colour) went with the oblique buildings
and the seam. `ui/ghost_seen.svg` stays in the repository but the game no longer draws it; only the
gallery's Ground page does, in its third ghost, as a sample of that retired look (dots alpha 0.7 in
#9AA5B1 over the sprite box, and the explored fog over them), until a gallery row redraws that
cell the way the layer does.

The gallery's `GHOST_*` constants hold the values above and `tests/art` pins this section to them;
the values are a proposal for touch-ux-dev to tune with Michael.

## Icons and glyphs
Icons live in `assets/art/svg/icons/`, named `<kind>_<name>.svg`, three kinds, all 128x128 with a
clear background. **Import scale 1.0 (new)**: an icon is drawn from 24 to 64 dp (42 to 152 base px
across the phone profiles), not in map tiles, so one sharp 128 px raster serves every size, as the
selection ring does. The world art (units, buildings, flags) keeps scale 0.5.
- `tab_<tab>`: the build tabs, Structures, Infantry and Vehicles, named by the tab.
- `item_<type_id>`: a build tile's icon, one per buildable type, named by P72's type id. The eleven
  types are the five buildings the player builds and the six units. The Headquarters is not
  buildable and has none.
- `emblem_<faction>`: a faction's emblem (P103: `emblem_fossil`, the Derrick League's; P104:
  `emblem_renew`, the Lumen Accord's), drawn like a glyph in greys and tinted at run time; the art
  id `FactionDef.emblem` names. See "Buildings".
- `glyph_<name>`: HUD, build menu, map and result glyphs. A placement reason's glyph is
  `glyph_reason_<reason>`, the `Placement.Reason` name in lower case (`outside`, `ground`,
  `occupied`, `unexplored`, `too_far`, `cap`); the view finds it by name. `OK` has none, and the
  "Blocked" of a pending ghost uses `glyph_reason_occupied`.

**Item icons** are in palette colours (a building icon in A's muted materials, a unit icon in the v1
palette until the sidebar reads the generated cards, P114), drawn like the sprites they were made
with (a unit top-down, a building in the oblique view of P91b's art, which P103 deleted, and which
the generated cards below replace) and simplified for a small size, with a 3 to 4 px edge in
#1C2127, thin inner details at 2 to 3 px and no drop shadow, on a clear canvas: the tile draws its
own panel. A unit carries one neutral grey (#9AA5B1, the palette's neutral) where its sprite has
team plates, and a building carries none: a build tile is not a team's.

**Generated item cards (P99b)** are the isometric successors of the hand-drawn item icons: one per
type of both factions, `assets/art/iso/icons/item_<type_id>.svg`, made by the generator from the
type's default view (see "Generated art"). They have their own plate and corner in the file (the
tile does not draw a panel for them) and keep their faction's key triad. The hand-drawn
`item_<id>.svg` above stay for the 12 merged types until the readers move (P114).

## Feedback markers (new)
The target flash, the attack-move marker, the long-press anchor and the box are drawn in code
(`_draw` or `Line2D`), not as SVG. They stay crisp at every zoom and are sized in dp (ADR 0004
section 7). The SVG technical rules do not apply to them, the palette does. The selection ring is
the exception (ADR 0011 section 3): one SVG texture, `ui/selection_ring.svg`, on a `Sprite2D` that
scales its baked stroke with the ring. touch-ux-dev builds the markers in P16 and the ring sprite in
P40, and pins the numbers below in tests.

Conventions:
- **dp** is `UiScale.dp(v)`, base px on screen. In world space (under `RtsCamera`) divide by the
  camera zoom, so a dp size looks the same at 0.75, 1.0 and 1.5. `MoveMarkerLayer` has no camera
  export, so it reads the zoom as `get_canvas_transform().get_scale().x`. **World px** (1 tile = 64)
  scale with the zoom, like the sprites.
- **Stroke**: two passes on one centre line, round caps and joins, antialiased. First the outline
  pass in #1C2127 at width + 2 dp, then the colour pass. 1 dp of dark shows on each side. That
  separates every colour from grass (luminance 127) and sand (186).
- **Fade**: full alpha until min(200 ms, `marker_ms` / 3), then linear to 0 at `marker_ms` (600),
  so a shorter `marker_ms` never makes the fade interval empty. Fade the outline pass and the
  colour pass as one group (a `CanvasGroup` per marker, or outline alpha a^2 under colour alpha a),
  because Godot blends each draw pass on its own and two plain alphas turn the marker dark mid-fade.
  **Ease** is ease-out quad, e(t) = 1 - (1 - t)^2.
- Alpha is 1.0 unless a row says so. Markers have no shadow and make no sound.

### Shape as second cue
- Colour is never the only cue (`touch-ui-rules`). Each meaning has its own shape: own selection is
  a closed circle around a unit, an enemy target is a closed diamond, attack-move is four open
  chevrons closing in, the box is a rectangle, and the long-press anchor is the only lightly filled
  ring and the only ring that shrinks in. A press on a unit puts the anchor on that unit, and on a
  desktop or tablet profile a unit ring can be as large as the anchor. Fill and motion tell the two
  apart there, not size.
- Test for every new marker: look at it in greyscale at the size the player sees. The marker
  colours have luminance white 255, warning 174, danger 120, outline 32. If two markers cannot be
  told apart by shape, size or motion alone, change the shape, not the hue.
- The sprites of team A and team B differ by hue only (luminance 116 and 120). In Phase 1 the markers
  carry the second cue: only own units get a ring, and a targeted enemy gets the diamond. Anything
  later that must tell the teams apart in a crowd (health bars, minimap dots) needs a shape
  difference.

default Arktisk (player 2), its hull and turret keeping their olive and metal. The sand half of the
gallery's zoom row shows the Flarejack so at 0.75, 1.0 and 1.5. Phase 1 targets stand still, so they
need no other cue.

## Life effects (plan row P135, ADR 0027)
The effects of the unit-life layers are two hand-written atlases, no team colour. Both are imported at
scale 0.5 like `fx/effects_atlas.svg`, so a region's numbers are px of the imported texture, which at
camera zoom 1 are world px, and the SVG is twice as large.
- `fx/life_atlas.svg`, 552 x 536 at 2x (276 x 268 px, about 24 KB): the sprites of `GroundFx` (z 3) and
  `AirFx` (z 25), the smokes, the flames, the sparks and the dust, and (P167, ADR 0029) the hit flash,
  chips, embers, gas jet, dust cloud and glow of the damage and hit layers.
- `fx/strike_atlas.svg`, 480 x 472 at 2x (240 x 236 px, about 10 KB): the sprites of `StrikeFx` (z 55), the
  two superweapon looks.

A layer draws a frame as a quad of its region, with `modulate` Color(1, 1, 1, alpha) and no other
tint (the colours are baked in), growing and fading as its own rows say (the numbers are in
`data/life_settings.tres`, not here). Each frame is authored at about the size a puff or a flame has
in the middle of its life, so a layer scales it between about 0.5 and 1.5 and stays soft but never
blocky. A layer never turns a quad. It may mirror a flame or a spark horizontally for a second
look (they give light, so nothing on them is lit from a side); it never mirrors a cloud, a heap of

**The net.** The Dusk Array's net is a lattice of `hex` cells on the ground ellipse of the strike's
radius: flat-topped hexagons of side 16 world px, so a column every 24 px, a row every 13.9 px
(13.86 exactly) and every other column shifted 6.9 px; neighbours share an edge, so a layer places
the cells at those exact distances (no rounding to whole px, or the two light lines of a shared
edge part and a dark seam shows between them) and their lines coincide in one. The net falls as
that lattice (the layer's own offset and scale), lands, and settles as the outer ring of cells with
the interior fading; `hex_lit` is the flash at the landing and `glint` the sparkle on its corners.
The lines of the net that the layer draws in code (ADR 0027 section 3) are a light line (#D8FFE0)
1.5 world px wide over a dark edge (#1F5C3A) 3 px wide, the widths of the sprite's own lines (a
thinner light line is under a pixel at zoom 0.75 and vanishes); `LifeRegions` holds the lattice
distances, the colours and the widths.

**The lance.** The collar is the same in both: its centre is at y 65 in `lance_down` and y 99 in
`lance_up`. The burn is the one part of the lance that is a flame; the body carries no fins and no
nozzle, and the flights are an auger's, so it never reads as a missile (the originality list bans a
missile out of silo doors and a column from orbit).

**Strips and joins.** `trail` and `seed_trail` are the two strips whose one end touches their
region's edge (the bottom of `trail`, the top of `seed_trail`, the anchor's end), so a layer
stretches them along the fall or the rise and the join with the sprite stays closed. `trail` joins
`lance_down` at 17, 65 (the collar's centre) and `seed_trail` joins `seed` at 9, 17: the layer puts
the strip's anchor on that point of the sprite and draws the strip first, so the sprite covers the
seam. A layer stretches a strip to at most 2 times its region's length (128 px), always fading out
at the far end, so the fall or the rise stays a short streak and never becomes a bright column from
the sky to the ground (the originality list bans a beam or column from orbit).

## Fog and terrain
`MapView` bakes the map once per match and per `map_version` (a terrain change) into one texture at
8 px per tile (ADR 0011 section 3) and draws it with `texture_filter` NEAREST, so a texel is 8x8
world px. The colours are `MapView.colors` keys: a terrain id of `data/terrain/standard.tres`, and
`<id>_b` and `<id>_c` for the second and third tone of its tile (for grass `_b` is the tile parity,
elsewhere a pattern tone). Probe is a texel (x, y) that shows the key's colour, for tests to read;
the tile centre would show the scrub's highlight, so it is named per key:

| Key | Colour | Where | Probe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

### Iso ground tiles (P105)
ADR 0023 sections 1, 5 and 6. When P116 lands, the ground is drawn from these diamonds. `MapView`'s
8 px bake above stays the source of the minimap's colours and of P52's keys (`TERRAIN_COLORS` and
`TERRAIN_TILES` keep every key and string), and its "no grid" rule stays for that bake and holds for
the diamonds too: they have no rim (below).
- **Files**: `ground/<terrain_id>_a.svg` and `ground/<terrain_id>_b.svg` for the six ids of
  `data/terrain/standard.tres` (grass, sand, scrub, rock, water, ore): 12 files, normal imports at
  scale 0.5, no team colour. A canvas is **128 x 64 at 2x**, so a tile is 64 x 32 px in game, one
  cell's diamond; its anchor is the canvas centre, the cell's centre. The variant is a fixed function
  of the cell, `(column + row) % 2` (a for 0), as the gallery draws it; the a and b of one terrain
  are two arrangements of the same objects and share their base and everything near the edge, so
  any mix of the two tiles together. `ore` is the stained earth under a field (see
  "Fields"), not a field stage.
- **Made as**: a small projection script kept outside the repository (as for the app icon; the
  committed SVG is the source, edit it by hand or write a new script). Details are drawn in ground
  coordinates, in tiles from the cell's centre, and projected with section 1's formulas, so a circle
  is a 2:1 ellipse. Elements are `svg`, `defs`, `clipPath`, `g`, `path`, `polygon`, `polyline` and
  `ellipse`, 2 to 4 KB a file, one decimal, no gradient. Each file starts with its two comments, the
  second `<!-- Pattern: ... -->`.
- **Seamless**: the base reaches 1 px (at 2x) past the diamond's edge, the ground square of 0.52
  tiles, which the canvas clips, so the antialiased edges of two tiles overlap and no hairline shows
  at zoom 0.75, 1 or 1.5; `tests/art` lays 3 x 3 blocks of mixed tiles at the three zooms and finds
  no pixel inside the block that is not opaque. The details are clipped to the ground square of 0.42
  tiles (a `clipPath` called `inner`) and are placed within 0.3 tiles of the middle, so a bush or
  a boulder is hardly ever cut.
- **No rim** (Michael, 2026-10-27, on the phone): the diamond is its base colour to the edge, with
  no band, line or shading near it. The first draft had a soft rim (ADR 0023 section 5): two bands
  of the tile's own base colour just inside the edge, 5 % and 2.5 % darker, 0.05 tile wide each. It
  read as a faint grid wherever two equal tiles met, so the two `path` lines that drew it are gone
  from all twelve files, and ADR 0023 section 5's soft rim is withdrawn (its note comes with
  P26ak). The seamless rule above holds as it did: the base reaches 1 px past the edge. Two equal
  tiles now meet with no line between them, and two different terrains meet on a hard edge, as the
  8 px bake's do. `tests/art` pins the base colour at 0.89, 0.93 and 0.97 of the way to the middle
  of each edge, in a and in b, and that no file has an `evenodd` band.

## Fields
ADR 0023 sections 2, 6 and 11; plan row P105 (P116 draws them). A field is the merged ore cells
(`ore_cell`, `ore_amount`, `ore_cell_max` 20): each cell is an `ore` ground diamond (`ground/ore_a`
or `ore_b`, the stained earth) with a **stage** drawn over it. The stage is view-only: **full** above
two thirds of `ore_cell_max`, **half** above one third, **dry** below, so a harvested cell steps down
and a regrowing one climbs back. **Oil or gas** is a view-only choice per field (the first cell's
function or `MapDef.field_kinds`, P116); both kinds pay the same. The art is drawn in the oil
and gas look of the canvas's resource board (a black pool with a sheen, a pale crust over a vent) and
re-authored; nothing is copied.
- **Files**: `fields/<kind>_<stage>.svg` with kind `oil` or `gas` and stage `full`, `half` or `dry`:
  six files of **128 x 64 at 2x** (64 x 32 px in game), normal imports at scale 0.5, anchored at the
  cell's centre like a ground tile, drawn in the fields-and-props layer (ADR 0023 section 2) over
  the ground. They are **overlays**: transparent outside the stain, nothing in the canvas corners
  and nothing past the diamond but the 1 px bleed of a full stage. Made like the ground tiles (a
  projection script outside the repository, ground coordinates, the committed SVG is the source).
- **Full runs together, half and dry stand apart**: a full stage fills its whole diamond (and 1 px
  more), so full cells make one lake or one crust, with no seam. A half or dry stage stops short of
  the middle of all four sides, so those cells are separate pools and patches with the earth between
  them, which reads as a drying field. `tests/art` pins both.

Michael, 2026-10-15: the game's look must stay replaceable as a whole without touching game rules
(plan `docs/plans/2026-10-11-phase-2.md`, "Answers and follow-ups (P26t docs)"). A new look is
made in three places, and the palette table above is the source for all three: change the
palette first, then the three places from it.
1. **Units**: the models `assets/art/models/<faction>/<type_id>.tres` and their generated frames
   under `assets/art/iso/` (see "Generated art"), found by the sheet of the art id
   (`assets/art/iso/index.tres`). Change the model and run `bash scripts/gen-art.sh`; never edit a
   frame. The team colour is the faction's key triad inside the SVG text, replaced by the player's
   at match start.
2. **Terrain**: the `colors` and `tiles` exports of `game/view/MapView.gd`, with the gallery's
   copies and the "Fog and terrain" section above.
3. **UI**: the central Theme of plan row P58, `game/ui/theme.tres`. It holds the HUD's fonts
   (Chakra Petch as the default font and for numbers; Big Shoulders Display ExtraBold as one
   `FontVariation` per size class of buttons and titles, P118), the font sizes, the selection
   panel's backdrop (palette row "HUD panel backdrop"), the HUD v2 tokens and the two accent pairs
   (the palette rows marked (v2)) and the colours of the selection box and anchor. The text and
   button colours and the Button StyleBoxes are the engine's defaults and are not in the file: a
   restyle adds them there (`Label/colors/font_color`, `Button/styles/normal`) with no code change,
   and gives the neutrals a palette row then. The file is written in dp, and `game/ui/HudTheme.gd` is the dp
   step: on a resize it gives the HUD roots a copy in px, scaling every font size and every
   StyleBox size (content margins, corner radii, border widths, expand margins, shadows, a line's
   thickness) but not a StyleBoxTexture's texture margins or the theme's constants (add one to
   `HudTheme.scaled()` before using it). An entry that stays in base px is listed in the file's
   `metadata/base_px`. The selection box and anchor take their fill and dark pass from the theme,
   and the world-space move markers from `FeedbackStyle` in `game/view`: they share one fill and
   one dark pass, so a new palette changes both places. A new font goes under
   `assets/art/fonts/<family>/` as the unmodified upstream file with the family's `OFL.txt`, with a
   row in `assets/CREDITS.md` (author, source URL, licence) and its licence text beside every build
   (P118b) and, from Phase 5's settings on, in its Licences screen, and is referenced only from the
   theme: `tests/project/test_ui_theme_scan.gd` flags a font named in any other script, scene or
   resource under `game/` and `scenes/`. The tracking of a Big Shoulders `FontVariation`
   (`spacing_glyph`, whole base px) is not scaled by the dp step and is listed in `metadata/base_px`;
   the three classes (button 22 dp with 4 px, title 28 dp with 5 px, big title 36 dp with 6 px) keep
   it at 7.1 to 10.3 % of the size on every phone profile of `ui_profiles.gd` (ratios 1.75 to 2.37;
   ADR 0025 section 13 asks 6 to 14 %), at 13.3 to 14.3 % on the tablet profiles (1.25) and at 16.7
   to 18.2 % in the desktop window (1.0, the dev window): one whole-px tracking cannot hold the
   band over ratios of 1.0 to 2.37, and the phones are what it is held for. The dp step reads only
   `font_sizes/` and `styles/`, so the three `*/fonts/font` entries in `metadata/base_px` are inert
   today and mark what a later step that scales fonts must leave alone. Chakra Petch has no
   OpenType `tnum` (its digits are proportional), so a number that changes keeps fixed digit slots
   in code (P119).
